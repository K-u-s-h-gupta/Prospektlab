0-3% FY26 constant-currency revenue guidance is the number that will shape the post-results debate around Infosys, even as the company’s operating margin stayed near 21% and its capital-return stance remained supportive. The market is now weighing whether large-deal wins can translate into faster growth, or whether uneven discretionary spending will keep the recovery gradual.

Infosys enters the new financial year with a mixed setup. On one hand, the company’s trailing operating margin of 20.9% and profit margin of 16.4% point to a business that has preserved profitability through a soft demand phase. On the other, the FY26 constant-currency growth outlook of 0-3% signals that management is not yet calling for a sharp acceleration in client spending. Retail sentiment has reflected that tension, with forum discussion described as mildly negative to cautious, mainly because guidance has not matched hopes for a stronger re-rating.

Analyst view

The key question for investors is not whether Infosys can defend margins in the near term, but whether deal wins can convert into revenue growth quickly enough to justify a better valuation. The stock’s trailing P/E of 14.6x and dividend yield of 4.5% suggest the market is still assigning value to cash generation and shareholder returns, but not yet to a full demand recovery. That leaves execution on large deals, attrition control and discretionary-spend trends as the main variables to watch.

Section 01

Why the market is focused on guidance

Infosys' Q4 FY25 numbers matter less for what they confirmed than for what they did not yet promise: a sharp demand rebound. The company's FY26 constant-currency growth guidance of 0-3% is modest, and that has become the central reference point for investors trying to judge the pace of recovery.

The stock's reaction is likely to hinge on whether clients resume discretionary spending more quickly than current guidance implies. For now, the message from management appears measured rather than exuberant, which can be reassuring on execution but limiting for near-term sentiment.

Retail commentary captured that tension clearly, with forum participants described as cautious and mildly negative. Their concern is not a collapse in profitability, but the possibility that growth may remain subdued for longer than the market had hoped.

Section 02

Margins remain a stabilising factor

Infosys' operating margin near 21% is important because it shows the company has preserved profitability even without a strong top-line acceleration. The TTM operating margin of 20.9% and profit margin of 16.4% suggest the business remains efficient by industry standards.

That resilience matters in a sector where wage costs, utilisation and project mix can quickly affect earnings quality. The current margin profile gives management room to absorb a slow recovery, though it does not eliminate the need for better revenue momentum.

Some forum posts questioned whether margin strength can last if demand stays uneven. That is a sentiment view rather than evidence, but it highlights the market's sensitivity to any sign that profitability could soften before growth improves.

Section 03

Large deals need to convert into revenue

The company's large-deal total contract value will be watched closely in coming quarters because investors want proof that wins are translating into billings and revenue. A healthy pipeline is useful, but the market typically rewards conversion, not just booking momentum.

This is where the current debate around Infosys is most practical. If deal wins begin to feed into execution and revenue growth, the 0-3% guidance range may prove conservative. If not, the market may continue to treat the stock as a steady compounder rather than a re-rating candidate.

That is why the next few quarters matter. The company has already shown it can defend margins; the open question is whether it can turn that stability into a clearer growth inflection.

Section 04

Capital returns help support the valuation

Infosys continues to benefit from a shareholder-friendly capital-return profile, and that has not gone unnoticed. Some retail participants were constructive on the company's balance-sheet strength and dividend support, even while remaining cautious on growth.

The valuation backdrop also reflects that balance. A trailing P/E of 14.6x does not suggest a deep discount, but it does indicate the market is still willing to pay for quality earnings and cash generation. The dividend yield of 4.5% adds another layer of support.

For investors, that means the stock is not being priced purely as a growth story. It is being assessed as a mix of stability, cash return and optionality on a demand recovery.

Section 05

What to watch next

The next read-through will come from commentary on client spending, deal conversion and attrition trends. Those indicators will help determine whether the current guidance range is a floor or a ceiling for FY26 growth.

If discretionary IT budgets improve, Infosys could see a better revenue trajectory without sacrificing margin discipline. If they do not, the company may continue to deliver steady profitability while the market waits for a stronger catalyst.

Either way, the stock's near-term direction is likely to be driven by evidence rather than expectation, and the evidence will come from execution in the quarters ahead.
